\begin{abstract}
Test feedback should help coding agents fix more bugs, yet under tight action caps a wide gap separates observing a failure from delivering a fix. Across 3,517 runs and shared-draft realizations in self-hosted Gemma 4 31B on 95 tasks from 15 repositories, we trace where feedback stalls before patch delivery. Reproducing bugs first raises target counterevidence by 54\% without improving net passes (75 vs 75). Shared-draft continuations show why: advance check outputs halve replay on failing drafts (72\% to 34\%) without adding retained edits (8 vs 8) or passes (3 vs 3), as agents trade saved test runs for file reads and 89\% hit their eight-call cap. Attrition spans four stages: broken heredoc launches (35\% on \texttt{python3 -B PY}), reading substitution (with 56 silent line-1 \texttt{read\_file} resets), cap-blocked first edits in 4 of 6 edit-attempting counterevidence branches (while landed edits pass), and delimiter corruption swallowing \texttt{old\_string:} in 93 edit calls or draft regressions. Across 2,565 follow-up runs, prompt-only caps, retry rules, and \texttt{keep}/\texttt{reject} gates fail, motivating runtime edit quotas, resilient tool adapters, and state-bound rollback.
\end{abstract}
